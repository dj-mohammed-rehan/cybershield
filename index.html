Frauds reported most often in India

These descriptions come from advisories published by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), CERT-In, and the Reserve Bank of India.

UPI and QR code scams

You are selling something online or expecting a refund. The other person sends a QR code or a collect request and says scanning it will credit money to you. Scanning and entering your UPI PIN does the opposite: it authorises money out of your account.

Warning sign: anyone who says you must scan a code or enter a PIN to receive money.

OTP and KYC fraud

A caller claims to be from your bank, wallet, or a telecom company and says your KYC is expiring or your account will be blocked. They ask you to share an OTP, or to install an app that lets them see your screen.

Warning sign: any caller who asks for an OTP, PIN, CVV, or asks you to install an app.

Phishing links

An SMS, WhatsApp message, or email carries a link to a page that looks like your bank, a courier, a power company, or a government portal. The page asks for card details, net banking passwords, or an OTP.

Warning sign: a link in an unsolicited message that opens a login or payment page.

Digital arrest scam

Callers pose as police, CBI, ED, or customs. They claim a parcel in your name held illegal goods, or your Aadhaar was misused, and keep you on a video call while demanding money to clear the case. No Indian law enforcement agency conducts arrests or settlements over a phone or video call.

Warning sign: any call claiming you are under arrest and must pay or stay on video.

Job and investment scams

You are offered part-time work such as liking videos or rating apps, with small payouts at first to build trust. Then you are asked to deposit money to unlock earnings. A related pattern uses fake trading apps or groups that promise guaranteed returns.

Warning sign: any earning scheme that first asks you to pay or deposit money.

Fake delivery and customer care

A message claims a package is held and asks you to pay a small fee or confirm details through a link. In a related pattern, a fake customer care number found through search or social media answers your call and walks you into an OTP or screen-sharing trap.

Warning sign: a support number that is not on the company's official website or app.

Ten rules that stop most fraud

Print these or save them. They cover the situations where nearly every common fraud begins.

  1. Never share an OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN, or CVV with anyone, including someone who says they are from your bank.
  2. Banks, wallets, and government agencies do not ask for these details on calls, SMS, or WhatsApp.
  3. Scan a QR code only to pay someone. You never need to scan a code or enter a PIN to receive money.
  4. Do not install any app because a caller asked you to. Screen-sharing apps hand over your phone.
  5. Do not trust a customer care number from a search result or social media. Take it from the official app or website.
  6. No police officer, CBI, ED, or customs official will arrest you, investigate you, or demand money over a call or video call. Hang up.
  7. Treat any message that creates urgency, threats, or guaranteed profits as a red flag. Pressure is the tool.
  8. Open links only from sources you can verify. When in doubt, type the official address yourself instead of tapping.
  9. Install apps only from the official Play Store or App Store, and check the developer name and review history.
  10. If money leaves your account in a fraud, call 1930 and your bank within the first few hours. Fast reporting improves the chance of freezing the money.

Where to report a fraud in India

These are the official channels. Keep transaction IDs, the phone number or account that contacted you, screenshots, and call records ready.

If money has just left your account, act in the first few hours. Call your bank to freeze the transaction, then call 1930. Quick reporting gives the system the best chance of holding the money before it moves further.

National cyber fraud helpline

1930

For frauds involving money: UPI, cards, net banking, wallets. Run by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C).

National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal

cybercrime.gov.in

File a written complaint online for any cyber crime, including frauds against women and children. You get a complaint number to track it.

Chakshu portal

sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc

Report suspected fraud calls, SMS, and WhatsApp messages, even before any money is lost. Part of the government's Sanchar Saathi initiative.
